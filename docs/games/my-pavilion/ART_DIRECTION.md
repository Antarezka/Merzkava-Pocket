# My Pavilion — Art Direction

## 1. Visual objective

My Pavilion uses a **stylized 3D fantasy presentation**. Characters should have chibi proportions with the visual identity and costume readability of MMORPG characters. Environments should feel like a detailed, modular fantasy commercial space, not a flat illustration or a tiny pixel-art map.

The chosen references establish a direction for evaluation. They do not mean that assets have already been purchased, imported, licensed, or approved for production use.

## 2. Character foundation — Serribaba

**Reference:** [Serribaba — Cute MMORPG Characters](https://serribaba.itch.io/cute-mmorpg-characters)

Use the Serribaba sample as the starting visual reference for:
- 3D chibi fantasy character proportions.
- Readable silhouettes and recognizable costume shapes.
- MMORPG-like identity through outfit, armor, accessories, and role.
- A character scale that can coexist with detailed furniture and architecture.

The final character standard still requires a project-specific review for:
- Head-to-body proportion and limb length.
- Face readability and expression range.
- Hair, fabric, armor, and accessory detail.
- Animation quality and rig flexibility.
- Outfit variation and material consistency.
- Suitable polygon, texture, skeleton, and animation budgets for the target device.

Do not reduce characters to tiny pixel sprites or replace the approved 3D direction with a 2D sprite workflow without explicit approval.

## 3. Environment foundation — Inventive World

**Reference:** [Inventive World — Alderwick Avenue, stylized modular street and shop interiors](https://www.artstation.com/marketplace/p/e8k06/alderwick-avenue-stylized-modular-street-and-shop-interiors)

Use this sample as the environment reference for:
- Modular shop and street construction.
- Interior layout and furniture density.
- Reusable architectural pieces and props.
- Distinct shop areas and recognizable commercial spaces.
- Consistent material language across walls, floors, furniture, and decoration.

The My Pavilion environment must be adapted to Merzkava's fantasy setting rather than copied as a modern street or ordinary retail shop.

## 4. Visual cohesion rules

Character and environment assets must be reviewed together in a representative scene.

- Keep character, counter, shelf, doorway, table, chair, stairs, and display dimensions mutually consistent.
- The pavilion must visibly read as a place designed for the character scale.
- Shelves and displays must not overwhelm characters or hide important goods.
- Keep materials and lighting coherent between characters, furniture, and architecture.
- Ensure goods can be visually distinguished when displayed in the pavilion.
- Maintain a clear silhouette and readable interaction pose at the actual gameplay camera distance.
- Use lighting and shadows to create depth without obscuring item information or making the interface hard to read.

## 5. Character animation requirements

The character rig should support modular costume variation and reusable animation sets where practical.

Minimum animation categories to evaluate:
- Idle: breathing, blinking, subtle weight shift.
- Movement: walking, turning, stopping, and carrying.
- Shop work: receiving boxes, moving goods, stocking shelves, arranging displays.
- Customer service: greeting, pointing, handing over an item, completing a transaction.
- Inspection: examining an object, comparing goods, reacting to an unusual discovery.
- Social behavior: conversation, waiting, sitting, celebrating, disappointment.

Animation quality must be judged in-game at the final camera distance, not only in a close-up model viewer.

## 6. Environment and prop categories

The asset plan should cover:
- Pavilion exterior, entrance, signs, and surrounding street.
- Interior architecture, floors, walls, doors, lighting, and partitions.
- Counters, shelving, cabinets, display cases, tables, storage, and work areas.
- Item display props, boxes, bags, containers, and transport equipment.
- Category-specific showcases for Arcanistry goods, minerals, medicine, poison, equipment, and loot.
- Background dressing that communicates the pavilion's location and status.

## 7. Asset intake and licensing

Before any referenced asset becomes a production dependency:
1. Identify the exact creator, pack, version, and source page.
2. Read the applicable license and redistribution restrictions.
3. Confirm whether commercial use, modification, and inclusion in a packaged Android application are allowed.
4. Record attribution requirements.
5. Keep a provenance record for every third-party asset.
6. If the license is unclear, do not ship the asset until clarified or replaced.

The reference pages are visual references only until their asset licenses and suitability are separately checked.

## 8. Art acceptance gate

A character/environment sample is accepted only after:
- It matches the chosen visual direction.
- Scale and proportions work together in a test pavilion.
- Materials, lighting, and silhouette are readable.
- Rigging and animation are usable in the runtime.
- Asset performance is measured on the intended device class.
- License and source provenance are recorded.

## 9. Decisions still open

- Exact character proportions and customization depth.
- Final material treatment: hand-painted, PBR stylized, or a controlled hybrid.
- Camera mode and distance.
- Lighting model and day/night presentation.
- Environment detail and performance budgets.
- Whether the final production uses the referenced packs, custom assets, or a combination.
