# My Pavilion — Game Foundation

## 1. Product definition

My Pavilion is a **3D fantasy trade-pavilion management simulation** set inside the Merzkava Universe.

The player's main responsibility is to operate a growing pavilion that buys, receives, evaluates, stores, displays, sells, and distributes world-relevant goods. The challenge is to keep the business supplied and trusted while demand, scarcity, quality, and supplier relationships change.

### Genre classification

- **Primary:** Business / shop management simulation.
- **Secondary:** Supply-chain and trade management; inventory and economic simulation.
- **Setting and presentation:** Fantasy management simulation with Merzkava worldbuilding.
- **Not the genre:** Trading-card game, card battler, pixel-art life simulator, or action MMORPG.

The game can contain character progression, exploration-derived goods, and narrative events, but the pavilion's trade operation remains the main gameplay focus.

## 2. Player role

The player is the operator and decision-maker of a trade pavilion. The player is not automatically the head of a particular canon clan, guild, empire, or Spirit Realm organization. The player's exact background and opening circumstances remain to be designed.

The pavilion should become useful to several kinds of customers and organizations rather than serving only one faction.

## 3. Gameplay pillars

### A. Trade and procurement
- Compare suppliers, prices, quantities, quality, reliability, and delivery conditions.
- Negotiate recurring supply agreements and limited contracts.
- Handle delayed, partial, damaged, substituted, or unavailable deliveries.
- Develop long-term supplier relationships.

### B. Item appraisal and provenance
- Identify item type, grade, quality, condition, origin, and authenticity.
- Distinguish an ordinary material from a rare or unusually valuable one.
- Keep lore descriptions consistent with the source material.
- Allow uncertain or suspicious goods to require further inspection rather than silently assigning perfect information.

### C. Pavilion and inventory management
- Arrange counters, shelves, showcases, storage, work areas, and customer spaces.
- Manage stock levels, item grouping, storage capacity, and display priorities.
- Expand or reorganize the pavilion as trade volume grows.
- Avoid a purely decorative building system: layout must support useful operations.

### D. Customers and demand
- Represent different customer needs, budgets, preferences, and levels of knowledge.
- Support walk-in purchases, requests, repeat customers, and special orders.
- Let demand respond to availability, price, reputation, local events, and relevant world conditions.
- Avoid making every customer behave identically.

### E. Distribution and contracts
- Receive goods from specialist organizations and independent suppliers.
- Sell directly to customers and fulfill bulk or contract orders.
- Track obligations, deadlines, quantities, and quality requirements.
- Make supplier and customer relationships matter to future opportunities.

### F. Business growth
- Improve facilities, storage, staffing, services, and supplier access.
- Build reputation through dependable transactions and fulfilled commitments.
- Unlock more demanding product categories and trade relationships over time.
- Expansion must introduce new management choices, not only larger numbers.

## 4. Initial commodity taxonomy

This is a starting taxonomy, not a complete or canon-locked item catalogue.

| Category | Example contents |
|---|---|
| Arcanistry goods | Artificial artifacts, instruments, components, specialist tools |
| Minerals and materials | Ores, crystals, stones, refined and unrefined materials |
| Medicine | Herbs, medicinal ingredients, pills, preparations |
| Poison and antidotes | Toxic ingredients, poisons, antidotes, specialist reagents |
| Spirit Beast materials | Hides, bones, organs, cores, other harvest materials where lore permits |
| Equipment | Weapons, armor, accessories, travel and field equipment |
| Expedition loot | Relics, discoveries, unidentified goods, recovered valuables |
| General trade goods | Everyday supplies and supporting commodities |

Every item added to the production catalogue must have a lore check before it is treated as a canon-compatible item.

## 5. Supplier and distribution network

Supplier groups are gameplay categories for now. Their final names, geography, hierarchy, and political relationships must be checked against Merzkava canon before being established as official factions.

| Supplier archetype | Potential specialty |
|---|---|
| Merchant guild / trade association | General merchandise, interregional trade, brokerage, contracts |
| Medicine-and-poison specialist clan | Herbal ingredients, medicines, toxins, antidotes, specialist preparations |
| Mining organization | Ore, stone, crystals, mineral materials |
| Arcanistry artisans or workshops | Artificial artifacts, instruments, components, commissioned goods |
| Hunter and expedition groups | Spirit Beast materials, recovered loot, field supplies, discoveries |
| Independent merchants | Variable goods, rare opportunities, irregular supply |

Supplier identity should affect available goods, reliability, price, minimum order, delivery time, and contract terms. A supplier is not just a reskinned shop catalogue.

## 6. Economy principles

The economy should be systemic rather than a collection of arbitrary fixed prices.

Relevant factors may include:
- Item category, grade, quality, condition, and provenance.
- Supplier availability and procurement cost.
- Stock on hand and storage limitations.
- Customer demand and willingness to pay.
- Regional access, travel or delivery constraints, and world events.
- Reputation, relationship status, contract obligations, and market competition.

No final pricing formula, currency conversion, inflation model, or time scale is locked yet. These must be designed and tested by the Economy Designer before implementation.

## 7. First playable experience — proposed scope

The first vertical slice should demonstrate one complete and understandable trade loop:

1. A small pavilion with a limited number of functional spaces.
2. A small set of clearly differentiated suppliers.
3. A representative catalogue spanning several item categories.
4. Delivery, receiving, appraisal, storage, display, and sale.
5. Customers with distinguishable needs and purchasing behavior.
6. A basic ledger showing revenue, costs, stock, and outstanding obligations.
7. A simple expansion or reputation milestone.

The vertical slice should prove the visual target and management loop before large-scale content production begins.

## 8. Open design questions

These decisions must not be silently assumed:
- Camera mode, rotation, zoom, and interior/exterior transition.
- Exact player character role and starting scenario.
- Whether customers and staff are directly controllable or primarily autonomous.
- Time progression and whether the simulation pauses.
- Pavilion scale, number of floors, and district/world travel.
- Extent of crafting, appraisal mini-games, or other hands-on activities.
- Multiplayer or online features; none are currently required or approved.
- Final economy formula, currencies, progression pacing, and endgame.
